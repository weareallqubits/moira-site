The Three Sisters

The Greeks believed that three sisters appeared three nights after you were born. They were the Moirai, and they came to determine the course of your life.

One sister spun the thread of your life into being. She was the beginning. The first breath. The moment you arrived in the world and the story of your life began to unroll. She held the spindle and drew the first fiber into existence. She was the one who said: this life exists. It begins here.

Another sister measured the thread. She determined its length and its texture. She was the middle. The living of it. The choices, the events, the relationships, the triumphs, the losses. She measured out the portion that was yours and assigned it to you. She was the one who said: this is your share. This is what you have been given to work with.

The third sister cut the thread. She was the end. The completion. She held the shears and decided when the story was finished. She was the one who said: this life is complete. The thread has been woven.

THE STORY OF MOIRA

Not Fate. Portion.

Here is the part that most people get wrong about the Moirai, and the part that matters most.

The Moirai are usually translated as "the Fates." That makes them sound grim. Inevitable. Doom. The idea that your life is already written and you are just walking a predetermined path, carrying your fate like a stone.

But that is not what the word moira meant.

Moira meant "portion." "Share." "Allotment." Your moira was your fair share of the harvest. Your portion of the land. Your lot in life. And the ancient Greeks understood something subtle and beautiful about this: your portion is given to you, but what you do with your portion is your own.

Homer describes moira as fate that "follows your steps and directs the consequences of your actions." She does not write the story. She gives you the thread. You weave it.

In the Odyssey, even Odysseus, the cleverest of heroes, had to live out his moira. But he also chose how to live it. He raged against the suitors. He loved his wife. He sailed home despite the gods. His moira was the journey. His choices were his own.

The ancient Greek understanding was this: you are given a portion. A share. A thread of a certain length and quality. The stars, the circumstances of your birth, the family you were born into, the era you live in, the gifts and challenges you carry. All of that is your moira. Your allotment. What no one can take from you and what no one can change.

But what you do with that portion? That is yours. The thread is given. The weaving is yours.

The Moirai gave you the thread. The weaving was always yours.
